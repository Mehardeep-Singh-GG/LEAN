## 1. Formal Mathematical Hypotheses

### Prediction P_M7_1: Knapsack Scaling Power-Law Exponent
Let a knowledge population consist of $K$ factual quanta where each quantum $i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ has:
1. Exposure frequency distributed according to a Zipf law:
   $$p_i = \frac{i^{-(1+\alpha)}}{\sum_{j=1}^K j^{-(1+\alpha)}}, \quad \alpha > 0$$
2. Constant description length cost $c_i = c_f = I_{\text{fact}} / \kappa$.
3. Constant marginal error reduction per exposure $\Delta_i = \Delta$.

Under the continuous knapsack allocation under total parameter bit budget $B$, the retained quanta are those with value-per-bit $r_i = p_i \Delta_i / c_i \ge \tau(B)$. The residual excess loss scales as:
$$L(B) - L_{\infty} = \sum_{i: r_i < \tau(B)} p_i \Delta_i \propto B^{-\alpha}$$
Specifically, the empirical power-law exponent $\gamma$ in $\ln(L(B) - L_{\infty}) = -\gamma \ln(B) + \text{const}$ satisfies:
$$\gamma \approx \alpha$$
For all three target regimes $\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.6, 1.0\}$, the empirical scaling exponent matches the theoretical prediction $\gamma_{\text{pred}} = \alpha$ within the 95% bootstrap confidence interval or within 20% relative error under discrete knapsack packing.

### Prediction P_M7_2: Frequency-Governed Fact Survival Monotonicity
Under budget-constrained compression, individual factual memories are discarded in reverse order of corpus frequency. For any fact $i$, the probability of survival at budget $B$ follows a logistic threshold:
$$P(\text{survives} \mid p_i, B) = \sigma\left(\frac{\ln p_i - \ln p^*(B)}{T}\right)$$
where $p^*(B) \propto B^{-(1+\alpha)}$ is the cutoff frequency and $T$ is the compressor temperature.
The Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC-AUC) predicting fact survival solely from training exposure frequency $p_i$ satisfies:
$$\text{AUC}(p_i \to \text{Survival}) \ge 0.80$$

### Prediction P_M7_3: Asymmetric Half-Life Ratio (Skills outlive Facts)
Because procedural skills have high usage frequency ($p_s \gg p_f$) and compact algorithmic program descriptions ($c_s = c_{\text{alg}} K(s)$), their value per bit satisfies $r_s \gg r_f$.
Consequently, under progressive quantization and pruning ladders, skills survive to substantially tighter bit budgets than factual associations.
Defining the half-life $B_{1/2}(X)$ as the bit budget at which capability $X$ drops to 50% accuracy:
$$\frac{B_{1/2}^{\text{fact}}}{B_{1/2}^{\text{skill}}} \ge 2.0\times$$
(Factual memory degrades at at least twice the bit budget of procedural skills).

### Prediction P_M7_4: Greedy vs. Exact Knapsack Optimality Bound
For large discrete capability sets ($N \ge 10^3$), the continuous greedy knapsack relaxation (sorting by $r_i = p_i \Delta_i / c_i$) achieves an objective value within 1.0% of the exact NP-hard integer programming knapsack:
$$\frac{V_{\text{exact}} - V_{\text{greedy}}}{V_{\text{exact}}} \le 0.010 \quad (\le 1.0\%)$$

### Prediction P_M7_5: Bounded Core Saturation under Compression
The algorithmic thinking core remains preserved under progressive compression until the bit budget approaches the theoretical minimum $B_{\text{core}} = c_{\text{alg}} \sum_{s \in S} K(s)$. For budgets $B \ge B_{\text{core}}$, the core description length remains bounded and functionally invariant.
