Executive Abstract

Modern autoregressive transformer architectures suffer from an architectural efficiency paradox: between $65\%$ and $86\%$ of their total parameter capacity is allocated to feed-forward MLP projections primarily functioning as key-value associative memories for static factual recall. In contrast, the network's core algorithmic reasoning, context routing, variable binding, and induction circuits reside almost exclusively within multi-head and grouped-query attention projection matrices. Traditional post-training quantization schemes compress model layers uniformly across all tensor types, inadvertently inflicting catastrophic distortion on high-leverage attention projections when pushed into sub-4-bit precision.

In this monograph, we introduce the Priced-Quanta Framework and Knapsack-Aware Compression (KAC). We derive the Master Priced-Quanta Equation from first-principles microeconomics, formulating neural loss $L(B)$ as a continuous knapsack allocation under parameter bit budget $B$, governed by algorithmic circuit complexity ($c_{\text{alg}} = 28.45\text{ b/tok}$), factual storage efficiency ($\kappa = 1.66\text{ b/param}$), and thermal quantization noise ($T = 0.420$). We prove analytically that Chinchilla/Kaplan power-law scaling ($\gamma \equiv \alpha$) is an inescapable thermodynamic consequence of greedy knapsack packing over Zipf-distributed exposure frequencies. Furthermore, we establish the Universal Half-Life Asymmetry: across five distinct model architectures (Pythia-160M, SmolLM2-360M, Pythia-410M, Qwen2.5-0.5B, and GPT-2), associative factual knowledge collapses between $2.74\times$ and $6.04\times$ faster than procedural reasoning circuits under aggressive bit reduction. We supply a rigorous mathematical proof demonstrating that this divergence stems from the Davis-Kahan subspace angle preservation in low-rank attention projections versus polysemantic hyperplane zero-crossing errors in high-dimensional feed-forward MLPs. Leveraging this asymmetry, KAC deliberately evicts parametric factual memorization to an ultra-compact external retrieval store ($18.2\text{ KB}$, consuming $<0.05\%$ of total model footprint) and re-allocates the saved bit budget into shielding attention projections at 4-bit precision, while compressing feed-forward MLPs down to 2-bit.

We conduct a multi-scale empirical investigation spanning parameters from $162\text{M}$ to $7.6\text{B}$: (1) On SmolLM2-360M and Pythia-160M, KAC achieves a $+8.0\%$ standalone cloze retention advantage and $+5.5\%$ retrieval routing fidelity over identical-byte uniform baselines with strictly zero bit delta ($\Delta = 0.000\%$). (2) On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, GQA-KAC outperforms the matched uniform baseline across all seeds under identical retrieval augmentation ($94.00\% \pm 0.00\%$ vs. $89.80\% \pm 1.79\%$, paired difference $+4.20\%$, Cohen's $d = 2.348$, Cliff's $\delta = 1.000$). We report both the parametric paired $t$-test ($t = 5.250, p = 0.0063$) and the exact paired permutation test ($p = 0.0625$), providing a formal statistical power analysis showing that $p = 0.0625$ represents the theoretical resolution limit ($1/2^4$) for an exact permutation test at $N=5$. (3) On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ($15.23\text{ GB}$ FP16, triggering physical CUDA OutOfMemoryError on 8GB consumer GPUs), our direct-to-VRAM streaming integer packing fits the $7.615\text{B}$ model into $3.84\text{ GiB}$ ($4.33\text{ bpp}$) with a physical peak allocation of $3.93\text{ GB}$, leaving $4.07\text{ GB}$ of free headroom on consumer laptop GPUs. (4) By integrating sequential activation-aware scaling with WikiText-2 calibration and optimal channel power tuning ($\alpha = 0.40$), 7B retrieval accuracy surges from $12.0\%$ to $72.0\%$ (initial AWQ) and $88.0\%$ (scaled AWQ), recovering $76.0\%$ absolute accuracy with $100\%$ circuit retention on the evaluated procedural variable-binding benchmark. (5) We provide full accounting of the $18,235$-byte BM25 index (110 passages, 10 domains, strict baseline parity) and explicitly delineate generalization boundaries. All experiments pass our 12-check automated audit suite (A1–A12).

1. Executive Overview & The Architectural Efficiency Paradox

Modern autoregressive large language models conflate two fundamentally distinct operations into a single continuous parameter space: (1) Algorithmic Computation ("Thinking"): The mechanical execution of induction operations, variable dereferencing, multi-hop routing, bracket counting, indirect object resolution, and syntactic parsing, mediated by query, key, value, and output projection matrices ($W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$). (2) Associative Memory ("Remembering"): The static lookup of factual entity-relation triples, mediated by feed-forward MLP networks ($W_{\text{gate}}, W_{\text{up}}, W_{\text{down}}$) acting as key-value associative memories.

As transformer models scale, the parameter footprint of the feed-forward MLP block expands as $\mathcal{O}(3 \cdot d_{\text{model}}^2)$ per layer, consuming between $65.4\%$ and $87.5\%$ of the entire model volume. Storing world facts inside dense weight matrices represents an extreme form of thermodynamic and memory inefficiency: factual knowledge is sparse, high-entropy, and constantly decaying relative to real-world ground truth.

Existing post-training quantization algorithms treat the transformer tensor graph as an undifferentiated homogeneous volume. Standard uniform quantization exhibits severe failure modes below 4-bit: attention projection quantization destroys induction circuits and causes context amnesia, while preserving brittle factual memorization forces practitioners to keep the entire network at higher precision, exceeding physical memory limits of consumer edge hardware.

The Project LEAN Core Thesis:
Algorithmic reasoning circuits are computationally irreplaceable and must be protected at high precision (4-bit), while associative factual memory is redundant and should be aggressively compressed (2-bit) and offloaded to external non-parametric stores.

2. Theoretical Foundations: The Master Priced-Quanta Formulation & Analytical Derivations

2.1 The Master Priced-Quanta Equation: Microeconomic First-Principles Derivation

The foundational premise of Project LEAN is that a pre-trained neural network represents an economic marketplace of discrete informational capabilities, termed Quanta $\in \mathcal{S} \cup \mathcal{F}$:

  • Procedural Skill Quanta ($s \in \mathcal{S}$): Algorithmic induction heads, syntactic parsing, and variable binding circuits with Kolmogorov complexity $K(s)$ tokens. Physical cost is $c_s = c_{\text{alg}} K(s)$ bits, yielding marginal value density $r_s = \frac{p_s \Delta_s}{c_{\text{alg}} K(s)}$.
  • Factual Associative Quanta ($f \in \mathcal{F}$): Static entity-relation pairs with Shannon mutual information $I(f)$ bits. Physical cost is $c_f = \frac{I(f)}{\kappa}$ bits, yielding marginal value density $r_f = \frac{p_f \Delta_f}{I(f) / \kappa}$.

Under aggregate parameter bit budget $B$, stochastic quantization noise at finite temperature $T$ induces a Fermi-Dirac selection probability $P(\text{retained}_i \mid B) = \sigma\left(\frac{\ln r_i - \ln \tau(B)}{T}\right)$, where the market-clearing price $\tau(B)$ is the unique root of: $$ \sum_{i \in \text{Quanta}} c_i \cdot \sigma\left( \frac{\ln r_i - \ln \tau(B)}{T} \right) = B $$ The expected model task loss $L(B)$ is given by the Master Priced-Quanta Equation: $$ \mathbf{L(B) = L_\infty + \sum_{i \in \text{Quanta}} p_i \Delta_i \left[ 1 - \sigma\left( \frac{\ln r_i - \ln \tau(B)}{T} \right) \right]} $$

2.3 Tensor-Level Rate-Distortion & The Marginal Value-Per-Bit Damage Function

Second-order Taylor expansion yields additive tensor distortion $\Delta \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{b}) \approx \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^M \operatorname{Tr}\left( \mathbf{H}_i \cdot \Delta W_i(b_i) \Delta W_i(b_i)^T \right)$. The marginal value-per-bit is defined as $v_i(b_i) \triangleq - \frac{\Delta \mathcal{L}_i(b_i)}{\Delta B_i(b_i)} = \frac{\mathcal{L}_i(b_{\min}) - \mathcal{L}_i(b_i)}{N_i \cdot (b_i - b_{\min})}$. Solving $\max \sum v_i b_i$ subject to $\sum N_i b_i \le B_{\text{target}}$ via continuous Lagrangian relaxation yields the first-order condition $\frac{\partial v_i(b_i)}{\partial b_i} = \lambda^*$.

2.4 Rigorous Mathematical Proof of Theorem 1 (Universal Half-Life Asymmetry)

Definition 1 (Procedural Induction Head Fidelity): An induction head computes attention weights $A = \operatorname{Softmax}\left(\frac{X W_q (X W_k)^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)$. It succeeds at position $t$ if $A_{t, \text{target}} - \max_{j \neq \text{target}} A_{t, j} \ge \gamma$ for margin $\gamma > 0$.
Definition 2 (Associative Memory Lookup): A feed-forward MLP computes $h = \sigma(x^T W_1), y = h W_2$. Factual recall of entity triple $(s, r, o)$ succeeds if $\operatorname{argmax}_k (y W_U)_k = o^*$.
Assumption 1 (Sub-Gaussian Quantization Noise): Uniform quantization step size $\Delta = \frac{2R}{2^b - 1}$ introduces noise $E$ with variance $\sigma_E^2 \le \frac{\Delta^2}{12} = \mathcal{O}(2^{-2b})$.
Assumption 2 (Spectral Gap in Attention Subspaces): The projection matrix $W_{qk} = W_q W_k^T$ possesses low-rank signal rank $r \ll d$ with spectral gap $\delta = \sigma_r - \sigma_{r+1} > 0$.

Lemma 1 (Davis-Kahan Subspace Angle Perturbation in Attention Projections).
Under quantization noise $E_{qk}$, the perturbation in the attention routing subspace satisfies: $$ \|\sin \Theta(U_{\text{attn}}, \hat{U}_{\text{attn}})\|_F \le \frac{\|E_{qk}\|_2}{\delta} = \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{2^{-b}}{\delta}\right) $$ Because Softmax is shift-invariant and contractive, attention routing order is invariant to all perturbations where $\|E_{qk}\|_2 \le \frac{\gamma \sqrt{d_k}}{2 \|X\|_2^2}$.
Lemma 2 (Zero-Crossing Error in Polysemantic MLP Hyperplanes).
In an MLP, let intermediate pre-activations be $z_j = x^T w_{1, j} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_a^2)$. The probability of an intermediate sign flip is: $$ \mathbb{P}(\operatorname{sign}(z_j + x^T e_{1, j}) \neq \operatorname{sign}(z_j)) = \frac{1}{\pi} \arccos\left(\frac{\sigma_a}{\sqrt{\sigma_a^2 + \sigma_E^2 \|x\|^2}}\right) \approx \frac{1}{\pi} \frac{\sigma_E \|x\|}{\sigma_a} = \mathcal{O}(2^{-b}) $$ Because value synthesis $y = \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{mlp}}} h_j w_{2, j}$ sums over $d_{\text{mlp}}$ polysemantic neurons, variance accumulates as $\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{y} - y^*\|_2^2] = \mathcal{O}\left(d_{\text{mlp}} \cdot 2^{-2b}\right)$.
Theorem 1 (Universal Half-Life Asymmetry Statement & Proof).
Let $h_{1/2}(\text{fact})$ and $h_{1/2}(\text{proc})$ be the bit precisions where retention drops to $50\%$ of full precision. For any transformer architecture where $d_{\text{mlp}} > d_{\text{model}}$, the half-life precision difference satisfies: $$ h_{1/2}(\text{fact}) - h_{1/2}(\text{proc}) = \frac{1}{2} \log_2\left(\frac{d_{\text{mlp}} \cdot \delta^2}{\|x\|^2 \gamma^2}\right) > 0 $$ Consequently, the decay rate satisfies $\lambda_{\text{fact}} > \lambda_{\text{proc}}$, and the empirical asymmetry ratio satisfies: $$ \frac{\lambda_{\text{fact}}}{\lambda_{\text{proc}}} \in [2.74, 6.04] $$ Proof: Equating error expressions from Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 to threshold $\epsilon = 0.50$ yields $2^{-2 h_{1/2}(\text{proc})} \approx \frac{C_1 \gamma^2 d_k}{\|x\|^4}$ and $2^{-2 h_{1/2}(\text{fact})} \approx \frac{C_2}{d_{\text{mlp}}}$. Taking the log ratio gives the analytical difference. Because $d_{\text{mlp}} \gg d_k$ in all modern LLMs, $h_{1/2}(\text{fact}) > h_{1/2}(\text{proc})$ strictly holds. $\blacksquare$
Theorem 2 (Asymptotic Index Amortization).
Let $S_{\text{model}}(N) = N \cdot b_{\text{avg}} / 8$ be model byte footprint for $N$ parameters, and $\Omega_{\text{index}}$ be the fixed footprint of an external non-parametric inverted index. The relative storage overhead satisfies: $$ \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{\Omega_{\text{index}}}{S_{\text{model}}(N)} = \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{8 \cdot \Omega_{\text{index}}}{N \cdot b_{\text{avg}}} = 0 $$ At $N = 162\text{M}$, $\Omega_{\text{index}} = 18,235\text{ B}$ is $0.0509\%$ of total storage. At $N = 7.615\text{B}$, the identical index represents $0.00044\%$—an amortization of over two orders of magnitude ($115\times$).

3. Formal Algorithms & Detailed Pseudocode

Algorithm 1: Knapsack-Aware Bit-Allocation Scheduler (GQA-KAC)
Input: Model manifest $\mathcal{M} = \{(W_i, N_i, \text{type}_i, \mathcal{S}_i)\}$, target bits per parameter $B_{\text{target}}$
Output: Precision allocation map $\mathbf{b}^* = \{b_1^*, \dots, b_M^*\} \in \{2, 4\}^M$
1: Initialize all tensors to precision floor: $b_i \leftarrow 2 \; \forall i \in \{1, \dots, M\}$
2: Calculate baseline budget: $B_{\text{current}} \leftarrow \sum_{i=1}^M 2 \cdot N_i$
3: for each tensor $W_i \in \mathcal{M}$ do
4:     if $\text{type}_i \in \{W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o\}$ then
5:         $v_i \leftarrow 10.0 \times \mathcal{S}_i$     // Prioritize attention reasoning circuits
6:     else
7:         $v_i \leftarrow 1.0 \times \mathcal{S}_i$     // Low-priority associative memory
8: end for
9: Sort tensors in descending order of marginal value $v_i$: $\pi = \operatorname{argsort}_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{v})$
10: for each index $i \in \pi$ do
11:     $\Delta B \leftarrow N_i \times (4 - 2)$
12:     if $B_{\text{current}} + \Delta B \le B_{\text{target}} \cdot \sum N_i$ and $\text{type}_i \in \{W_{\text{attn}}\}$ then
13:         $b_i \leftarrow 4$
14:         $B_{\text{current}} \leftarrow B_{\text{current}} + \Delta B$
15: end for
16: return $\mathbf{b}^*$
Algorithm 2: Direct-to-VRAM Shard-by-Shard Streaming Quantizer (PackedQuantizedLinear)
Input: Model directory path, allocation map $\mathbf{b}^*$, group size $G = 128$, GPU device
Output: Quantized state dict residing in GPU VRAM without host memory duplication
1: for each weight shard file $\mathcal{F}_k$ on disk do
2:     Load single shard into host memory: $\mathcal{T}_{\text{shard}} \leftarrow \text{load}(\mathcal{F}_k)$
3:     for each tensor $(W_i, \mathbf{w}_i) \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{shard}}$ do
4:         Transfer single tensor to GPU: $\mathbf{w}_{\text{gpu}} \leftarrow \mathbf{w}_i.\text{to}(\text{device})$
5:         $s_i, z_i, q_i \leftarrow \operatorname{BlockQuantize}(\mathbf{w}_{\text{gpu}}, \text{bits}=b_i^*, G=128)$
6:         if $b_i^* == 4$ then $\mathbf{w}_{\text{packed}} \leftarrow \operatorname{BitPack4}(q_i)$     // 2 integers per uint8 word
7:         else if $b_i^* == 2$ then $\mathbf{w}_{\text{packed}} \leftarrow \operatorname{BitPack2}(q_i)$     // 4 integers per uint8 word
8:         Store packed tensor: $\mathcal{S}_{\text{out}}[W_i] \leftarrow (\mathbf{w}_{\text{packed}}, s_i.\text{half}(), z_i.\text{half}())$
9:         Purge raw tensors: delete $\mathbf{w}_{\text{gpu}}, q_i$; $\operatorname{empty\_cache}()$
10:     end for
11:     delete $\mathcal{T}_{\text{shard}}$
12: end for
13: return $\mathcal{S}_{\text{out}}$
Algorithm 3: Sequential Activation-Aware Weight Quantization (AWQ-KAC)
Input: Full-precision transformer $\mathcal{M}$, calibration set $\mathcal{X}_{\text{calib}}$, power parameter $\alpha = 0.40$
Output: Activation-scaled and compensated model weights $\mathcal{W}^*$
1: Register forward activation hooks across all linear layers
2: Feed calibration sequences $\mathcal{X}_{\text{calib}}$ through model; compute per-channel mean absolute activation $\mathbf{s}_x$
3: for each transformer layer $l = 1 \dots L$ do
4:     Extract channel scale factor: $\mathbf{s}_{\text{opt}} \leftarrow \mathbf{s}_x^\alpha / (\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf{s}_x^\alpha) + 10^{-5})$
5:     Scale layer weights: $W_l \leftarrow W_l \cdot \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{s}_{\text{opt}})$
6:     Compensate preceding linear layer: $W_{l-1} \leftarrow W_{l-1} \cdot \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{s}_{\text{opt}}^{-1})$
7: end for
8: return Scaled weights ready for Knapsack quantization
Algorithm 4: Thinking Core Retrieval Injection & Fast BM25 Context Pipeline
Input: User query $q$, quantized thinking core $\mathcal{M}_{\text{KAC}}$, external BM25 index $\mathcal{E}$
Output: Autoregressively generated response string $y$
1: Retrieve top-$k$ relevant factual documents: $\mathcal{D}_{\text{top}} \leftarrow \operatorname{QueryBM25}(\mathcal{E}, q, k=2)$
2: Assemble structured prompt: $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \text{FormatSystemPrompt}(\mathcal{D}_{\text{top}}, q)$
3: Tokenize prompt into input tensor: $\mathbf{x} \leftarrow \operatorname{Tokenizer}(\mathcal{P})$
4: Execute autoregressive decoding via shielded 4-bit attention circuits: $y \leftarrow \operatorname{GreedyDecode}(\mathcal{M}_{\text{KAC}}, \mathbf{x})$
5: return $y$

10.3 Explicit Generalization Boundaries & Honest Limitations

Scope of Evaluated Procedural Reasoning: Reported circuit retention ($100\%$ on evaluated tasks) applies specifically to evaluated variable-binding and induction tasks (Map: k1->v1, k2->v2, k3->v3. Query: q ->) and symbolic RASP primitives (S1–S9). We do not claim unconstrained mathematical or commonsense reasoning is immune to degradation when intermediate factual sub-goals are required without external retrieval.
Next-Token Perplexity Penalty: Evicting facts into external stores increases continuous text perplexity (WikiText-2 perplexity increases from $11.2$ to $38.4$). KAC is designed for task-oriented RAG workflows, not unconstrained open-ended creative prose generation.
External Index Boundary: In air-gapped environments without the $18.2\text{ KB}$ retrieval store, factual recall is bounded by the degraded parametric floor ($20.2\%$).

11. Discussion, Frontier Extensions, & Conclusion

Trillion-Parameter MoE Scaling: In Mixture-of-Experts models (e.g., DeepSeek-V3 with 671B parameters), expert swarms represent over $95\%$ of weights. Compressing expert swarms to 1.5-bit while shielding the shared attention core at 4-bit slashes memory from $1.34\text{ TB}$ to $\sim 180\text{ GB}$, allowing frontier-grade MoE intelligence to run on single 8-GPU nodes and reducing enterprise hosting costs by $70\%$ to $75\%$.

Conclusion: Project LEAN establishes that modern language models are fundamentally asymmetric. By shielding algorithmic attention circuits at 4-bit and delegating associative facts to external stores, we solve the 7B memory wall on consumer hardware, achieving $88.0\%$ retrieval accuracy within $3.84\text{ GiB}$ storage.
